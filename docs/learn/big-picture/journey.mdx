# A book's journey

<OneSentence>
A book is downloaded once and prepared for the panel, kept in a folder on the reader, drawn page
by page into memory, pushed to the e-ink screen, and its progress goes back to the server.
</OneSentence>

Before looking at any one part of Inkshelf, follow a single book all the way. Each stop on the way
is a chapter of this site; this page is the map that ties them together.

<Analogy icon="🚂">
Think of a train line. The book is the passenger; each station is a part of the code that does one
job and hands the book on. Two kinds of passenger take slightly different tracks: comics (pictures
of pages) and EPUBs (text that must be laid out).
</Analogy>

## Two kinds of book, two roads

The big difference between a comic and an EPUB is **when the hard work happens**.

- A **comic** is a stack of pictures. Turning a big color picture into the panel's 16
  <Term id="grayscale">grays</Term> is slow on the reader's processor, so Inkshelf does it once,
  at download time, and saves each page as a small <Term id="pg4">.pg4</Term> file. Turning a page
  later only means reading that file back.
- An **EPUB** is <Term id="reflowable" />: its text has no pages until you choose a font size.
  So Inkshelf keeps the EPUB as it is, notes where each chapter starts (`index.json`), and lays
  out pages only when you read them.

Recipes from Mealie take the comic road: the app lays a recipe out into page pictures, which are
then prepared like a comic's. A <Term id="fixed-layout" /> EPUB does too: its pages are drawn
whole at download time.

## Why so much happens at download time

Three constraints shape the trip, and you will meet them again in every part:

1. **The reader is slow.** A page turn must feel instant, so anything that can be done in advance
   is: page images are prepared while downloading, and the pages around the one you read are
   prepared in the background.
2. **The panel is slow too.** An e-ink refresh takes a visible moment, and fast refreshes leave
   <Term id="ghosting" />. So Inkshelf refreshes only the area that changed, and every few pages
   flashes the whole screen clean.
3. **Wi-Fi comes and goes.** A download can stop halfway, so files are written whole or not at all
   (<Term id="atomic-rename" />), and `book.json` comes last. Progress waits on the reader until the
   server can take it.

<details>
<summary>Going deeper: what the shelf folder looks like</summary>

Each book has a folder named after its id, under `inkshelf/servers/<server name>/books/` on the
reader:

| A book of pages (comic, recipe, fixed-layout EPUB) | A reflowable EPUB |
|---|---|
| `0001.pg4`, `0002.pg4`, … one per page | `book.epub`, the file as the server sent it |
| | `index.json`, its chapters and table of contents |
| | `cover.pg4`, its cover, prepared for the panel |
| `book.json`, written last | `book.json`, written last |

A CBZ is first downloaded whole, its pages are prepared into `.pg4` files, then the archive is
deleted: only the pages take space.

The EPUB's page count in `book.json` is a <Term id="virtual-page" /> count: 1500 characters a page
(`CharsPerPage`), since the real pages depend on the font size.

</details>
